The spare bit, by hand

She sends 0. The process flips it, so the desk is shown 1. The spare page is 1. XOR of shown bit and spare is 0, and the copy stores the sent 0. Reading the stored 0, flipping it, and XORing with the stored page brings the spare back to 1. The same steps work if the process is the constant that always returns 0, and if the process is the identity. No count of fixed points is used. Adding a bit twice is the bit. That is the whole open map.

Send 0, flip, spare 1, store 0. Reverse: read 0, flip to 1, XOR with the stored page, spare returns.

What the diagonal is counting

Once the letter is fed back, the diagonal counts strings that equal their image under the identity intervention. A process function has one such string for each intervention, not eight for the identity intervention. The copy rule has one for every message. On three desks those are 000, 001, 010, 011, 100, 101, 110, and 111. Off the diagonal, two strings differ. The witness is the desk where they differ as outputs and agree in what the process returns. Orthonormal rows make that desk a zero. Drop the rows and the zero is not forced. The temporal file records the eight. It does not record the zero.

Lewis, Novikov, Ori

Lewis is about the word can. A small change of her hand would have stopped the trip, so in that sense she can. The afternoon that occurs is one in which she does not, so in that sense she cannot. The split does not pick the afternoon. Novikov keeps the consistent data and gives the rest measure zero. Earman says a selection rule is not a dynamics. The process-function rule is a condition on the map, which is closer to a dynamics and is not a measure.

Ori's 2007 model is a compact vacuum core, a dust shell of non-negative density, and closed null geodesics at the boundary of the future domain of dependence of a compact set. It is a background on which the question can be asked. Dust follows geodesics. It does not evaluate the fixed-point equation. The wormhole billiard has data with one continuation, two, or none. That is the same trichotomy for one ball. It is not the three-bit function. The 1995 action paper is Carlini, Frolov, Mensky, Novikov, and H. H. Soleng.

The embedding that exists is the discrete bijection. It is not a line element.
