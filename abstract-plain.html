What this paper is about

Imagine a letter that arrives at four and has to be the letter you write at five. You may write whatever you like. Some policies make no consistent afternoon: you try to write the opposite of whatever arrived, and no page can be both the thing you received and the opposite of the thing you will send. Other policies make two afternoons: you copy, and a blank page and a page of ink both work. The paper is about the policies that leave exactly one afternoon, no matter what each person decides to write.

Those policies are called process functions. The grandfather story is the policy with no afternoon. The story of the note that was always in the coat is the policy with two. Neither is thrown out by saying it is rare. It is not a policy of this kind.

The paper then asks whether such a policy can be written as a reversible quantum circuit. The bookkeeping step can. You add a spare page, copy the afternoon onto it, and scramble the letter with that spare page. Running the scramble backwards returns the spare page. This works for every policy, including the bad ones. It is only a way of keeping the books reversible.

Feeding the letter back into the desk is a second step. For the policy where each person copies, that step counts eight consistent afternoons on three desks, not one. So the closed step is not reversible for every policy. For a process function it is. If two afternoons both completed, some desk would have sent different letters and been shown the same letter, which a single rule cannot do. That desk makes the extra afternoon cancel. The closed circuit is then reversible, which is what the paper means by unitary.

Nothing here builds a wormhole, or a solution of Einstein's equation, that implements the three-desk rule. The wormhole papers are cited as the older problem, not as a construction of this map.
